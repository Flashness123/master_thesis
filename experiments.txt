- Observed: in the multi-level model an action changes the embedding very little.
  - The distance to the goal differs by only ~2 between actions, against ~23–37 in the single-level model.
  - Consecutive states are ~6× closer (copy loss) and the effect of an action is ~50× smaller (action gap).
  - The distance to the goal hardly changes from one step to the next (e.g. 308 → 308 → 309, see the per-step table).
- Observed: measured by rank, the two models point in the right direction about equally often.
  - The real next states rank the human action best 40 % of the time for both.
  - Strangely, the single-level model's 1-step prediction ranks the human action worse than random (mean rank 3.45), and it still plans successfully. So the 1-step rank doesn't explain planning success: the planner scores 5-step sequences (closest approach to the goal), not single moves.

Interpretation (hypothesis, not tested)

SIGReg pushes the embeddings of each batch towards a spread of 1 in every direction. When a batch mixes 6 levels, most of that spread is used to tell the levels apart, which differ in their whole layout. The player's position inside a level, the thing that matters for navigation, gets only a tiny part of the embedding.

The direction information is still there (40 % rank-1, like the single-level model), but it's ~15–20× weaker. It is then probably drowned out by prediction errors, so CEM finds no consistent direction. This fits the observations so far: the prediction loss looks good, the planner hovers near the start, and level 3 never works.
